Transmission Fluid Intervals: Manual vs Automatic vs CVT

Transmission fluid controls heat, lubrication, and friction quality. Service timing has a direct impact on shift quality, clutch wear, and gearbox life.

What Changes Service Intervals

  • Operating temperature: frequent high heat shortens fluid life.
  • Driving profile: towing, hills, traffic, and aggressive acceleration increase stress.
  • Gearbox design: CVT and dual-clutch systems can be more fluid-sensitive.
  • Service history: unknown history usually justifies a conservative interval.

Practical Interval Baseline

Gearbox TypeNormal UseSevere Use
Manual60,000-90,000 km40,000-60,000 km
Automatic (torque converter)50,000-80,000 km30,000-50,000 km
CVT40,000-60,000 km25,000-40,000 km
Dual-clutch50,000-70,000 km30,000-50,000 km

Decision Framework

  1. Confirm exact OEM fluid specification and service method.
  2. Classify your usage as normal or severe (traffic, towing, steep routes).
  3. Inspect shift quality, fluid color, odor, and historical invoices.
  4. Choose interval by risk reduction, not by minimum maintenance spend.

FAQ

Is lifetime transmission fluid really lifetime?

In practice, no. Heat, shear, and contamination degrade fluid over time, especially in stop-and-go or towing use.

Should I do a flush or a drain-and-fill?

For many high-mileage vehicles with unknown history, a staged drain-and-fill is lower risk than an aggressive flush.

Does CVT fluid need specific standards?

Yes. CVTs are sensitive to fluid friction properties. Always use the exact OEM-spec fluid.
